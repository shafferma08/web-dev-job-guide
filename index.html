Start with the path that fits you

Junior web developer openings are scarce right now, and many ask for a degree or years of experience. The graduates who get hired usually do three things at once: get free help with their résumé, show up where local developers gather, and apply to nearby roles as well as developer roles.

Year Up United, Tampa Bay

About a year long: classroom training first, then an internship at a large employer. Tampa Bay currently offers two tracks, Banking and Customer Success. Neither is a developer track, but both are a paid way into large companies that also have tech teams. Where web skills get hired locally

Marketing and web agencies

Small agencies in St. Petersburg, Clearwater and Tampa hire juniors and freelancers for WordPress work. Many never post the job. Meet them at local meetups, or send a short email with your portfolio link.

Schools, cities and counties

Public employers post web, IT support and communications roles on their own job boards. Search each one for “web”, “IT” and “digital”. Some developer postings require a degree, so read the requirements.

Staffing firms

Robert Half, TEKsystems and Vaco all place web and IT contractors in Tampa Bay. A recruiter there costs you nothing, and contract work is often how the first job happens.

Avoid scams. Real employers do not charge you. Walk away from any “job” that asks you to pay for training or equipment, cash a check and send money back, or interview only by text message.

Portfolio and skills

For a junior developer the portfolio does the work a degree does for other applicants. A hiring manager gives it about a minute.

What to show

  • Three finished projects are better than ten half-done ones.
  • Each project needs a live link, a link to the code on GitHub, and two or three sentences on what it does and what you built.
  • Include at least one project that solves a real problem for a real person or business.
  • List the certifications you earned in your program, with the exact names.

Check before you send it

  • It works on a phone.
  • Every link works and nothing says “coming soon”.
  • Images have alt text and you can reach everything with the keyboard.
  • Run it through PageSpeed Insights and fix what it flags.
  • Your name, email and LinkedIn are easy to find.

Keep building while you search

Gaps worry employers less when your GitHub shows recent work.

  • Frontend Mentor: build real designs and get feedback.
  • freeCodeCamp: free courses and certifications in JavaScript and more.
  • The Odin Project: a free full-stack curriculum with projects.
  • MDN Learn: the reference professionals use.

Real experience without a job

Build or fix a site for a local nonprofit, a family business or a community group. Treat it like client work, with a short write-up of the problem and the result, and it belongs on your résumé as experience.
